This detailed information is typically found in unstructured text notes from electronic health record (EHR) systems or other ancillary systems. Extracting such information manually is costly, not scalable, and far too slow to address current needs. OBJECTIVES: Our general objective was to develop and assess a new software application for COVID-19-related information extraction based on the same new natural language processing (NLP) methods to improve the accuracy, validity, and efficiency of information extraction from EHR text proposed in our current PCORI award. A new reference standard (sometimes called a gold standard and commonly used for evaluating system performance) of clinical text notes annotated with COVID-19-related information was created. The guideline is also available in Appendix A. The INCEpTION annotation tool was adapted and used as a secure web application for clinical note annotation with COVID-19-related information. The annotated collection includes 75 clinical notes from both institutions (ie, the Medical University of South Carolina and Vanderbilt University). We built the new NLP-based tool (named DECOVRI for Data Extraction for COVID-19-Related Information) on a standard framework based on Apache UIMA (Unstructured Information Management Architecture) and used open-source resources to ease dissemination. The current release of DECOVRI includes preprocessing modules for accurate detection and classification of sections and splitting of text into sentences and tokens (ie, words). To extract COVID-19-related information, we combined 3 major methods for finding and extracting concepts from unstructured notes: a dictionary lookup and 2 machine learning methods (specifically, conditional random fields and bidirectional long short-term memory deep neural networks). Our tool also performs local context analysis of all extracted information to determine whether the extracted information is currently true and about the patient (as opposed to historical data, negated data, and data about someone other than the patient). It exports the results to a common data model, the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership. Common data models define standards for stored data and relationships across those data, which facilitates sharing of the results with other investigations and institutions. To assess the accuracy of DECOVRI, the testing subset from the reference standard was used and the tool output compared with the reference standard annotations. Note: UMLS licensing constraints prevent direct release of the lexicons used in this project. Instructions for generating project dictionaries for lexical lookup are available via DECOVRI's GitHub page and can be used in tandem with lex_gen, a freely-available tool developed by our team. The annotation guideline was developed and implemented in a secure web-based annotation tool at both sites. The annotation process by domain experts was delayed but eventually completed with good intermediary interannotator measurements. The new NLP tool, DECOVRI, was developed and first released in May 2020 as a beta version including the preprocessing and dictionary lookup modules. An initial accuracy evaluation based on a small set of 15 clinical notes resulted in a micro-averaged F1-measure of 0.7254 and a macro-averaged F1-measure of 0.8316. The higher macro- than micro-average indicates strong performance across classes and weaker performance on higher frequency classes. In short, DECOVRI was developed and released as a tool available to other researchers and institutions to extract COVID-19-related signs and symptoms for downstream uses such as automated symptom monitoring or cohort creation. Performance, based on a sample data set created during this grant, indicates DECOVRI performs better across a wide range of concepts than on just the most frequent concepts. CONCLUSIONS: Despite delays in the development of a new reference standard of clinical notes, an initial prototype version of a novel NLP tool was developed in record time and then progressively improved with new or revised components, allowing for consistent and accurate information extraction. TITLE: Development of a privacy preserving large language model for automated data extraction from thyroid cancer pathology reports
ABSTRACT: Background Popularized by ChatGPT, large language models (LLM) are poised to transform the scalability of clinical natural language processing (NLP) downstream tasks such as medical question answering (MQA) and may enhance the ability to rapidly and accurately extract key information from clinical narrative reports. However, the use of LLMs in the healthcare setting is limited by cost, computing power and concern for patient privacy. In this study we evaluate the extraction performance of a privacy preserving LLM for automated MQA from surgical pathology reports. Methods 84 thyroid cancer surgical pathology reports were assessed by two independent reviewers and the open-source FastChat-T5 3B-parameter LLM using institutional computing resources. Longer text reports were converted to embeddings. 12 medical questions for staging and recurrence risk data extraction were formulated and answered for each report. Time to respond and concordance of answers were evaluated. Results Out of a total of 1008 questions answered, reviewers 1 and 2 had an average concordance rate of responses of 99.1% (SD: 1.0%). The LLM was concordant with reviewers 1 and 2 at an overall average rate of 88.86% (SD: 7.02%) and 89.56% (SD: 7.20%). The overall time to review and answer questions for all reports was 206.9, 124.04 and 19.56 minutes for Reviewers 1, 2 and LLM, respectively. Conclusion A privacy preserving LLM may be used for MQA with considerable time-saving and an acceptable accuracy in responses. Prompt engineering and fine tuning may further augment automated data extraction from clinical narratives for the provision of real-time, essential clinical insights.

TITLE: Diagnosing psychiatric disorders from history of present illness using a large-scale linguistic model
ABSTRACT: Aim: Recent advances in natural language processing models are expected to provide diagnostic assistance in psychiatry from the history of present illness (HPI). However, existing studies have been limited, with the target diseases including only major diseases, small sample sizes, or no comparison with diagnoses made by psychiatrists to ensure accuracy. Therefore, we formulated an accurate diagnostic model that covers all psychiatric disorders. Methods: HPIs and diagnoses were extracted from discharge summaries of 2,642 cases at the Nara Medical University Hospital, Japan, from 21 May 2007, to 31 May 31 2021. The diagnoses were classified into 11 classes according to the code from ICD-10 Chapter V. Using UTH-BERT pre-trained on the electronic medical records of the University of Tokyo Hospital, Japan, we predicted the main diagnoses at discharge based on HPIs and compared the concordance rate with the results of psychiatrists. The psychiatrists were divided into two groups: semi-Designated with 3–4 years of experience and Residents with only 2 months of experience. Results: The model's match rate was 74.3%, compared to 71.5% for the semi-Designated psychiatrists and 69.4% for the Residents. If the cases were limited to those correctly answered by the semi-Designated group, the model and the Residents performed at 84.9% and 83.3%, respectively. Conclusion: We demonstrated that the model matched the diagnosis predicted from the HPI with a high probability to the principal diagnosis at discharge. Hence, the model can provide diagnostic suggestions in actual clinical practice. © 2023 The Authors.
